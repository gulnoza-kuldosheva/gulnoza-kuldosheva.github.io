Research

Property wealth, who owns it, and who gets access to it.

Working papers

  • Birth origin, employment, and time to first property ownership in Luxembourg

    Draft available on request.

    Abstract

    Real estate is the main component of household wealth in most developed countries, yet who becomes an owner, and how quickly, is less well understood, particularly for foreign-born residents. This paper studies time to first residential property ownership in Luxembourg, using an individual-level administrative panel that links social security records to the national property register over 2002–2022. Using Cox proportional hazard models, I find that all foreign-born groups enter ownership more slowly than Luxembourg-born residents. Employment explains part of this gap, but not in the same way for every group: for Portuguese-born residents, who have high employment rates but low wages, controlling for employment reveals a larger underlying disadvantage. Among the employed, contract type matters strongly: temporary workers enter ownership at roughly one quarter the rate of workers on permanent contracts.

  • The distribution of property wealth in Luxembourg: new evidence from comprehensive register data (2002–2022)

    With V. Dautel, F. Docquier, A. Paccoud and E. Peluso. LISER Working Paper, 2025.

    Summary

    Using merged social security and land registry data covering the full population, the paper documents the level and concentration of real estate wealth in Luxembourg. Aggregate real estate wealth more than doubled between 2007 and 2022 and remains highly concentrated: the top 1% holds over a third of total property wealth, and widespread non-ownership is the main driver of this inequality.
